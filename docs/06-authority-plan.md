# Authority and links

*Written 2026-09-07.*

---

## The rule this plan is built on

**Earn links, never buy them.** Buying links violates Google's spam policies,
and the penalty is applied to the site that received them. For a business whose
entire goal is being found in search, a manual action is not a setback — it is
the loss of the thing being built.

That rules out: paid link packages, guest-post networks, PBNs, bulk directory
submission, comment and forum spam, and anything sold as "500 backlinks for ₹X".
There is no version of these that is worth the risk, and the cheapness is the
tell.

What follows is slower and it does not evaporate at the next algorithm update.

---

## 1. Links this business can actually earn

Ranked by how achievable they are for a young studio.

### Project credits — the most reliable source

Every client site is a potential link. Offer a discreet footer credit —
"Website by Skilloura" linking home — as part of the handover, and put it in the
written scope so it is agreed rather than assumed.

- Ask, do not assume. A credit added without discussion gets removed.
- Some clients will decline. That is fine and it should cost them nothing.
- These are the highest-quality links available here: genuinely relevant,
  genuinely earned, and they accumulate with every project.

### Professional and platform profiles

Real profiles with a real link, most of which take an hour once:

- **GitHub organisation** — the repo is already public. For a technical studio
  this is credible in a way a directory listing is not.
- **LinkedIn** — company page and the founder's profile
- **Behance / Dribbble** — the concept builds are genuinely presentable work
- **Clutch, GoodFirms, DesignRush** — free listings; buyers comparing studios use them
- **Product Hunt** — only if something genuinely launch-worthy ships

### Local and community

- **Bhubaneswar / Odisha chamber of commerce or IT association** — a real local
  link, and the local relevance is worth more than the domain authority
- **Local business associations** in whatever sector a client is in
- **College and alumni pages** — genuine, and often surprisingly strong domains
- **Sponsoring a local meetup or student event** — small money, real link, real
  relationships

### Content that gets referenced

This is the slowest route and the one with the highest ceiling. What earns a
link is something nobody else has:

- **Original measurement.** The site already measures its own builds under
  throttled conditions and publishes the numbers with their conditions. Very few
  Indian studios publish anything like it. A write-up of *what real Indian
  small-business sites actually score on Core Web Vitals*, measured across fifty
  of them, would be genuinely citable.
- **Honest price transparency.** "What a website actually costs in India, from
  someone who publishes their prices" is a piece nobody selling ₹2 lakh builds
  will write.
- **Technical guides** on the things this studio has actually solved — the RLS
  and publishable-key problem, the ISR publish-visibility gap, measuring LCP
  without fooling yourself. These attract developer links rather than buyer
  links, which is a different and useful audience.

The test for any of these: **would someone link to this without being asked?**
If not, it is content, not an asset.

## 2. What not to do, and why

| Tactic | Why not |
|---|---|
| Paid links / guest post networks | Direct policy violation; manual action risk |
| Bulk directory submission | Useless at best, harmful at worst |
| Comment and forum link drops | Nofollowed, and it damages reputation with the audience you want |
| Reciprocal link schemes | Detected trivially, discounted entirely |
| Buying expired domains | Long-term liability for a short-term number |
| Automated outreach at volume | Burns the domains and inboxes you would later want |

---

## 3. What to measure

Not the raw link count — that is the number sellers optimise for.

- **Referring domains, not links.** A hundred links from one directory is one
  domain.
- **Relevance.** A link from an Odisha business association beats a link from a
  generic high-authority directory.
- **Referral traffic.** A link that sends nobody is worth less than it looks.
  Search Console → Links, and GA4 → Acquisition.

Check quarterly, not weekly. This moves slowly and watching it closely only
produces anxiety.

---

## 4. Realistic expectations

For a studio at this stage, **five to ten genuinely relevant referring domains
in the first year is a good outcome**, and most of them will come from client
credits rather than from any outreach campaign.

That is a smaller number than link sellers will tell you is necessary. It is
also the number that is real, and for local and long-tail queries — which is
what §1 of `01-keyword-strategy.md` says this business should be targeting — it
is enough. Local rankings are won far more by the Google Business Profile, on-page
relevance and reviews than by link volume.

The sequence that actually works: **do good work → ask for the credit → publish
something worth citing → repeat.** There is no shortcut that survives contact
with a Google update.
